# PAPER-2019-MACWAN-MAICA-RPPG

## identity

- title: Heart rate estimation using Remote Photoplethysmography with Multi-objective Optimization
- authors: Richard Macwan, Yannick Benezeth, Alamin Mansouri
- year: 2019
- venue: Biomedical Signal Processing and Control 49, 24-33
- doi: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bspc.2018.10.012

- evidence_status: PROVISIONAL

## research_question

Improve ICA-based rPPG by converting blind source separation into semi-blind extraction using prior periodicity, reducing the need for heuristic component selection. See abstract/introduction, pages 1-3.

## method

- Proposed method: MAICA, multi-objective optimization using autocorrelation and ICA.
- Objective combines negentropy and mean squared autocorrelation to select a periodic rPPG component. See Section 3, pages 6-9.
- RGB traces are generated by spatial averaging face-cropped or skin-segmented pixels.
- Face detection/tracking: Viola-Jones plus Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi in MATLAB.
- Skin detection: Conaire et al. skin detection.
- Detrending: smoothness priors with regularization parameter `lambda = 500`.
- HR estimation: highest FFT peak in acceptable range `F in [0.7, 3] Hz`.
- Windowing: 30 s moving window with 0.5 s step for UBFC-RPPG; 15 s window for MMSE-HR due to shorter videos.
- Smoothing: Kalman filter with constant velocity model; motion and measurement noise variances set to represent about 1 BPM standard deviation.
- citation_anchor: System Framework, pages 9-10.

## capture

- UBFC-RPPG SIMPLE: 9 videos, about 21k frames, subjects relaxed and closed eyes.
- UBFC-RPPG REALISTIC: 46 videos, about 94k frames, time-sensitive mathematical game.
- Camera: Logitech C920 webcam, about 1 m from subject.
- Resolution: 640 x 480, 8-bit uncompressed RGB.
- FPS: 30.
- Lighting: ambient light with limited illumination variations.
- MMSE-HR: 97 usable videos, about 105k frames, 25 fps, varied skin colors.
- citation_anchor: Experiments and Results, pages 10-11.

## evaluation

- Metrics: MAE, SNR, Pearson correlation; PRECIS 2.5 and PRECIS 5 also defined as percent of windows within 2.5 or 5 bpm.
- Comparison methods: ICA, PCA, Green, CHROM, POS, G-R.
- citation_anchor: Table 1, page 13.

## results

- On UBFC-RPPG REALISTIC, MAICA MAE 3.34 bpm, r 0.89; CHROM MAE 3.81 bpm, r 0.87; POS MAE 4.73 bpm, r 0.80; ICA MAE 6.16 bpm, r 0.76.
- On UBFC-RPPG SIMPLE, MAICA MAE 0.55 bpm, r 0.99; POS MAE 0.67 bpm, r 0.99; CHROM MAE 0.72 bpm, r 0.99.
- On MMSE-HR, MAICA MAE 3.91 bpm, r 0.86; ICA MAE 5.28 bpm, r 0.70; CHROM MAE 5.59 bpm, r 0.83; POS MAE 5.77 bpm, r 0.82.
- Green performed poorly in this setup: MAE 9.86 bpm on SIMPLE, 7.73 bpm on REALISTIC, and 10.65 bpm on MMSE-HR.
- citation_anchor: Table 1, page 13.

## limitations

- MAICA is more computationally expensive than ICA because autocorrelation objective is also calculated. See discussion, page 13.
- UBFC-RPPG had limited illumination variation; MMSE-HR was not originally designed for rPPG and lacks PPG waveform ground truth.
- The authors note periodic motion can confound periodicity-based extraction. See Conclusions, page 15.

## reproducibility

## evidence_updates

- Adds evidence that 30 s windows are a common accuracy/stability tradeoff for offline HR estimation.
- Adds evidence that simple Green-only baselines may fail under realistic settings and must be quality-gated.
- Adds MAICA as a candidate method, not an MVP default.

## proposed_experiments

- Implement MAICA only after GREEN/CHROM/POS benchmark harness is stable.
- Add periodic-motion confound fixtures because MAICA may lock onto nonpulse periodicity.
- Compare raw window estimates against Kalman-smoothed estimates with explicit latency and artifact tradeoffs.
